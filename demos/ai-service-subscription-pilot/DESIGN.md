## Taste Brief

- Audience: merchants first, payment integration experts second
- Product personality: credible, calm, evidence-led, and exploratory without looking like a developer console
- Density: concise customer story by default; denser technical evidence on demand
- Typography goals: Fraunces provides a warm editorial display voice while Source Sans 3 keeps commerce, usage, account, and evidence content highly legible; runtime font proof remains pending
- Imagery direction: functional diagrams, state traces, allowance indicators, and restrained product illustration; avoid decorative AI imagery that competes with payment learning
- References: shadcn/ui primitives, the approved visual-companion interaction studies, and familiar AI chat/usage patterns
- Explicit reject list: default shadcn appearance, low-contrast text, decorative gradients without semantic purpose, fake PSP buttons, hidden provider constraints, and visually forced PayPal/Stripe parity

## Approved Direction

The approved product interaction direction is a coherent customer subscription story with a separate but connected Integration Lab. The lab has Scenario Builder, Matched Compare, and Capability Matrix tools joined by a persistent scenario context. Results default to a merchant narrative and reveal technical detail on demand.

The selected customer-facing structure remains the C3-A AI Atelier Plan Gallery: four service tiers and detailed service-capability mapping appear before payment selection. C3-G now owns the surface treatment: restrained G1 editorial glass in light mode and G3 aubergine glass in dark mode. Glass creates depth for navigation, workspace panels, cards, drawers, and overlays; high-opacity evidence islands preserve prices, balances, warnings, payment and tax evidence, recovery state, forms, tables, and provider provenance. PayPal, Stripe, wallets, Link, and other payment routes remain checkout choices rather than tier features, and provider-controlled components retain their official appearance. The approved component route assigns one clear job to shared primitives, limits data-rich components to their owning surfaces, and deliberately defers components without a current user-state need. These selections do not bypass the pending responsive-state, accessibility, reduced-transparency, or runtime-verification gates.
